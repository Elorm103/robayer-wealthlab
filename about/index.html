Meet the founder

Robert Loh Kobla

Founder, Robayer WealthLab

Robert Loh Kobla is a laboratory technologist by profession, an entrepreneur by passion, and a quality assurance professional by practice.

He currently works as a Quality Assurance Executive with a leading cGMP pharmaceutical company in Accra, where his work involves quality systems, compliance, documentation, auditing, procedures, and making sure that things are done properly, not simply assumed to be right.

His professional journey has also taken him through roles as a teaching assistant, laboratory assistant, and research assistant: experiences that shaped something else that would eventually become central to Robayer WealthLab, the ability to take something complicated and make it understandable.

But Robert did not start Robayer WealthLab because he was a financial expert.

He started it because he realised that too many ordinary Ghanaians were being left out of the conversation about money.

The question that started it

It began with a simple question.

Are you investing?

Robert started asking colleagues and people around him a simple question.

The answers were revealing.

Some had never heard of the Ghana Stock Exchange in any meaningful way. Others had heard of Treasury bills but did not really understand how they worked. Some knew the words mutual funds, shares, bonds, investment platforms, dividends, brokerage accounts, and money market funds, but had no idea how these things actually fitted together.

And many had quietly reached the same conclusion: investing is for people who already have money.

That assumption is one of the biggest barriers to financial participation.

So Robert started researching. Not for a weekend. Not by watching a few motivational videos and calling himself an expert.

Months of reading, comparing, questioning, learning, and researching followed: hundreds of hours of it.

What became increasingly clear was that there was a knowledge and communication gap between many ordinary Ghanaians (students, graduates, young professionals, national service personnel, and first-time earners) and the financial opportunities available around them.

The problem was not that people did not care. Many simply did not know where to start.

Where financial education goes wrong

Sometimes, information is simply introduced from the wrong place.

A lot of financial education seemed to begin somewhere in the middle.

It reminded Robert of something he knew very well from the laboratory.

You would not teach a new Quality Control analyst how to use an HPLC by beginning with a chromatogram and assuming they already know everything that happened before the peaks appeared.

You would first show them the instrument. Explain what it does. Show them the important parts. Show them where to switch it on. Then teach them how to use it properly.

You do not hand someone the keys to a Ferrari and start explaining engine diagnostics when they have not learned how to drive.

Financial education should not work that way either.

People should not have to understand ten financial concepts before someone finally explains the first one.

Start where the learner is. Then build from there.

That became the philosophy behind Robayer WealthLab.

Why we exist

We believe people deserve to understand what they're doing.

Robayer WealthLab was founded to bridge that gap. Not by pretending that everyone needs to become a financial professional. Not by drowning beginners in jargon. And certainly not by promising guaranteed wealth.

We believe people deserve to understand what they are doing before they are expected to make decisions about their money. That is why our guides are built around practical questions:

  • What is this?
  • How does it work in Ghana?
  • Where do I start?
  • What do I need?
  • What does this term actually mean?
  • What happens after I take the first step?

And perhaps most importantly:

"What does this mean for someone like me?"

And because our guides are designed specifically around the Ghanaian context, we are not simply taking generic financial education and changing a few words. We are asking: how does this actually work here?

About Robert's financial background

Let's be completely transparent.

Robert is not a chartered accountant.

He is not presenting himself as a licensed investment adviser.

He does not claim to replace accountants, licensed financial professionals, fund managers, brokers, tax professionals, or other specialists.

And he does not need to pretend otherwise.

His role is different. He is a researcher, educator, practitioner, and entrepreneur who recognised a problem and built a platform to solve it.

Robayer WealthLab is about making financial concepts understandable, practical, and accessible to people who may be encountering them for the first time.

As the platform grows, appropriately qualified professionals, including chartered accountants and other specialists, will be brought on board where their expertise is required.

A different kind of expertise.

His background is different. That does not make Robayer WealthLab less credible. It explains why the platform exists, and why it teaches from the beginning rather than the middle.

Good financial education doesn't require one person to know everything. It requires knowing what you know, knowing what you don't, and knowing who should handle what comes next.
So, why learn here?

You don't need someone pretending to be ten years ahead of you.

Sometimes you need someone who has done the reading, asked the questions, broken down the complicated parts, and is willing to walk through them with you.

That's what Robert set out to build.

  • A place where a student can ask a question without feeling embarrassed.
  • Where national service personnel can learn about investing before earning their first serious salary.
  • Where a graduate can finally understand what a brokerage account is.
  • Where someone starting with GH₵100 doesn't feel that investing is a conversation reserved for millionaires.
  • Where "I don't understand this" is not the end of the conversation. It's the beginning.
