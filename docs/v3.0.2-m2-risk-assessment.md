# Version 3.0.2 - Sprint M2A - M2 Risk Assessment

Planning only. Every risk below is assessed against the current, real production codebase and its actual constraints, not a hypothetical one.

---

## Technical risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| `pdf-lib` does not run cleanly inside the `workerd` runtime (untested assumption) | Medium | Medium | Required spike on day one of M2B (Receipt Architecture Plan), with a documented, ready-to-use HTML-receipt fallback that ships the same customer value without a true PDF. Never discovered mid-implementation - discovered before the real service is written. |
| Adding PDF generation to the synchronous webhook fulfilment path slows down webhook response time or introduces a new failure mode inside an already-proven, idempotent pipeline | Medium | Medium | Follow the exact discipline `fulfilPurchase()` and M1's customer-provisioning block already established: wrapped in its own try/catch, logged on failure, never allowed to affect the payment-verification outcome already recorded. If the spike shows PDF generation is slow enough to matter, generate it lazily on first download request instead of eagerly at webhook time - a design choice to make with real timing data from the spike, not guessed now. |
| `order_items.quantity > 1` (the seat-cardinality/ADR-009 loop) has no real production data to exercise it - every live product today is single-item | Low-Medium | Medium | A dedicated integration test with a synthetic multi-quantity purchase (see Testing Strategy) is mandatory, not optional, precisely because production data cannot be relied on to catch a bug in this path. |
| Receipt total (`receipts.total_pesewas`) silently drifts from `purchase_sessions.amount_pesewas` due to a computation bug | Low | High (a wrong receipt is a real financial-record integrity problem, not a cosmetic bug) | An application-level assertion at receipt-creation time (not a DB CHECK, since SQLite cannot cross-reference another table in a constraint) that throws/logs loudly if the two ever disagree, plus a dedicated test asserting equality on every fulfilment test case. |
| The new `receipt_download_tokens` table duplicates `download_tokens`'s pattern instead of extending it, risking future maintenance drift between two near-identical token tables | Low | Low | Deliberate, documented trade-off (Database Planning Report) - the alternative (weakening `download_tokens`'s existing NOT NULL FK) was judged a worse risk to a live, security-critical table. Acceptable, monitored, not a defect. |

## Business risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| VAT-registration status remains unanswered, blocking the tax-computation logic specifically (not the schema, not the receipt feature as a whole) | Medium-High (already true today, unresolved across multiple prior sprints) | Medium | Flag to the business owner in parallel with implementation start, exactly as the Sprint Readiness Report already recommends - "begin schema and non-tax-computation work immediately." M2B should ship with the tax-computation function returning an honest empty/zero breakdown until answered, never a guessed rate. |
| A real customer receives a receipt with an incorrect or legally non-compliant tax treatment because the VAT question was guessed rather than confirmed | Low (only if the mitigation above is ignored) | High | Do not guess. The empty-array degenerate case is explicitly valid per ADR-008 and is the correct default until the business fact is confirmed - guessing a rate would be worse than showing no tax breakdown at all. |
| The refund/revocation scope-boundary decision (Architecture Compliance Report) is left unresolved into implementation, causing M2B to either under-deliver against the ratified success criteria or over-build a full refund workflow not asked for | Medium | Medium | Explicit Product Owner sign-off on the recommended resolution (minimal admin revoke action, full `refund_requests` deferred) before M2B begins - named as an action item in the Implementation Roadmap. |

## Deployment risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Worker deployed before the migration, referencing tables that do not yet exist in production | Low (M1's own deployment already proved the discipline works) | High | Identical order-of-operations already proven for migration 0018: migration first, Worker second, static site third. Document this explicitly in M2's own Production Readiness Checklist, following the exact template M1's checklist already established. |
| R2 storage cost/growth from storing a generated PDF per receipt, indefinitely (receipts are "never deleted" per the Data Governance section) | Low | Low | Receipt PDFs are small (a few KB to low hundreds of KB, similar order of magnitude to the flagship eBook's own cover image assets already in R2) - not a meaningful cost concern at this project's realistic transaction volume. Worth a one-line note in the eventual go-live review, not a blocker now. |
| Static-site deployment step (GitHub Pages, same mechanism M1's deployment discovered and used) is assumed available without re-confirming, and something about the repository/Pages configuration has changed since M1 | Low | Low | Re-verify the mechanism at M2B deployment time with the same read-only checks M1's deployment used (`git status -sb`, checking for a `.github/` workflow) rather than assuming M1's finding is still accurate without a fresh look. |

## Migration risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| The new migration accidentally alters or drops something existing, rather than staying purely additive | Low | High | The Database Planning Report's entire design is additive-only by construction (four new tables, zero `ALTER TABLE` statements) - the same discipline migration 0018 already proved out safely in production. Code review should explicitly check for the absence of any `ALTER`/`DROP` before this migration is applied. |
| Applying the migration to production fails partway through (partial application) | Low (D1/SQLite migrations in this project's tooling apply as a sequence of statements, and 0018's own 17-command application completed cleanly) | Medium | Same rollback posture as 0018: leaving a partially-additive schema in place is inherently low-risk since nothing depends on it until the corresponding Worker code deploys after it. Re-run `wrangler d1 migrations list --remote` immediately after applying, exactly as M2's Production Readiness Checklist should require, to confirm clean completion before proceeding to the Worker deploy step. |

## Performance risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| `GET /api/customer/purchases` becomes slow as a customer's purchase history grows | Low at current scale, worth designing correctly now | Low-Medium | Pagination from day one (already specified in the API Planning Report), backed by the existing `idx_purchase_sessions_customer` index (already live since M1) - no new index needed for the base query; add `idx_order_items_session`/`idx_licenses_customer`/`idx_receipts_customer` (already planned) to keep the joined lookups indexed too. |
| Eager PDF generation inside the webhook path adds latency to Paystack's webhook round-trip, risking a timeout on the provider's side | Medium (depends on the spike's real timing data) | Medium | Directly informs the "eager vs. lazy" generation decision named in the Technical Risks row above - if the spike shows meaningful latency, generate lazily on first download request instead, which keeps the webhook path exactly as fast as it is today. |

## Overall risk posture

No risk identified above is severe enough to block starting M2B. The two risks worth resolving *before* implementation begins, specifically because they are cheap to resolve now and expensive to discover mid-build, are: the `pdf-lib` compatibility spike, and explicit sign-off on the refund/revocation scope boundary. Both are named as day-one action items in the Implementation Roadmap.
