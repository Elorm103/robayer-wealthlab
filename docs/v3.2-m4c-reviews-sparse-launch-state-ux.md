# Version 3.2 - Milestone M4 - Reviews Sparse/Empty Launch-State UX Design

**This document resolves M4B's Required Amendment #3** (`v3.2-m4b-architecture-approval-report.md`: "M4C's UX planning must explicitly design Reviews' empty/sparse launch state"), written retroactively at M4E closeout. M4D's independent acceptance review (`v3.2-m4d-amendment-compliance-report.md`) found that the underlying behavior shipped correctly at M4C but that this design reasoning was never written down as its own traceable artifact - unlike Amendments 1 and 2, which each had a dedicated resolution document from the start. This document closes that specific gap. See [`v3.2-m4c-amendment-tracking-correction.md`](v3.2-m4c-amendment-tracking-correction.md) for the full accounting of why this amendment was missed in M4C's own numbering.

---

## The Requirement

`v3.2-m4b-product-strategy-review.md`'s own framing, verbatim: "with only 2 real customers and 17 purchases, a newly-launched Reviews feature will very likely start with zero or one review... A product page showing 'No reviews yet' is honest and consistent with this project's established AR-009 empty-state discipline, but the Board recommends this be stated explicitly as a design requirement... not left implicit."

Two distinct things were required: (1) the correct *behavior* (an honest, non-manufactured-urgency empty state, matching AR-009), and (2) an explicit, written *design decision* saying so, rather than leaving it to be inferred from the shipped copy.

## The Design Decision (What Was Actually Built)

`backend/routes/books.ts`'s reviews section renders one of three states, decided purely by `reviewsResult.count`:

1. **Zero approved reviews**: `<p class="text-secondary mb-4">No reviews yet — be the first to share what you thought.</p>` - plain, honest, muted-color text. No fabricated star ratings, no placeholder testimonials, no "Be the first!" urgency framing beyond a simple invitation. The section heading itself falls back to plain "Reader reviews" (no star glyphs, no fabricated average) rather than showing a misleading `0 out of 5`.
2. **One or more approved reviews**: the aggregate heading renders real stars and a real computed average (`renderStars(reviewsResult.averageRating ?? 0)` + `"X out of 5 (N reviews)"`), followed by the actual review cards.
3. **The write/edit form** (client-rendered, `js/components/product-reviews.js`) is present regardless of how many reviews already exist - a signed-out visitor always sees a "Sign in to write a review" prompt, a signed-in customer with a verified purchase always sees the submission form, independent of whether any other review yet exists on the page.

This is a direct, deliberate application of AR-009 (this project's own "no manufactured urgency, no fabricated social proof" discipline, already established sitewide - see, for example, the Trust Signals section's real, checkable claims rather than invented testimonials). No new pattern was invented for Reviews; the existing discipline was applied to a new surface.

## Why This Is the Right Design, Not a Placeholder

- **Honesty over persuasion.** A product with two real customers and a handful of purchases genuinely may have zero reviews for a long time. Showing a fabricated "5.0 (127 reviews)" or a hidden section would misrepresent the product's real state - the opposite of this platform's own stated trust posture (see `v3.2-m4b-product-strategy-review.md`'s own observation that Reviews exists specifically to address "zero trust/social-proof mechanism exists in code").
- **The section is never hidden.** Even at zero reviews, the "Reader reviews" section renders (with its honest empty-state copy) rather than being conditionally omitted - a hidden section would look like a missing feature, not an honest signal.
- **No engagement-manufacturing trick.** No countdown, no "3 people are viewing this," no synthetic seed reviews. Consistent with this project's explicit governance history of rejecting exactly this class of dark pattern.

## The Known, Accepted Limitation This Design Decision Sits Alongside

M4B's Customer Journey Validation separately and specifically required a related disclosure: Reviews (M4) ships before the automated review-request trigger (M5) that would prompt customers to actually write one. Between M4 and M5, a review can only be left if a customer proactively navigates back to a product page they've already purchased - no post-purchase email or dashboard prompt exists yet. This is now recorded explicitly in [`v3.2-m4c-known-limitations.md`](v3.2-m4c-known-limitations.md) (added at M4E closeout, closing the second half of this same M4B requirement). The sparse-launch-state UX design above is the correct, honest way to present the product *while* that gap exists - not a workaround for it.

## Verification

Live-browser confirmed (M4D's own review, re-confirmed at M4E): a freshly-seeded zero-review product renders "No reviews yet — be the first to share what you thought." with no console errors, and the write-review form correctly renders a working sign-in prompt for a signed-out visitor beneath it.
