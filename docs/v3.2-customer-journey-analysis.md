# Version 3.2 - Sprint M4A - Customer Journey Analysis

Walks the full customer lifecycle as it actually behaves in production today (verified against real code, not aspiration), naming every gap found at each stage.

---

## Visitor

**Today:** Lands on the homepage or a `/books/*`, `/blog/*`, `/resources/*`, or `/free-guide/` page, all real, D1-rendered content. Client-side category-pill + live-search filtering exists on blog/resources listings.

**Gaps:**
- No unified search across content types - a visitor searching "budget" has no single place to find the relevant ebook, blog post, *and* free resource together.
- No social proof anywhere on a product page (no reviews, no ratings, no "N people bought this").
- No visitor analytics of any kind exist to even measure where visitors drop off before purchase - this is a measurement gap, not just a UX gap, and it means every claim about "the biggest UX risk" (the M3A Risk Assessment's own repeated flag: "the account-creation prompt's conversion impact is wrong in a way only real usage reveals") is currently unverifiable in either direction.

## Browse

**Today:** Product catalog is real (`products`/`product_files`/`product_gallery`/`product_relations`), fully D1-sourced, admin-manageable.

**Gaps:**
- No recommendations ("customers who bought this also bought...") - `product_relations` table exists but is admin-curated only, not behavior-driven.
- No wishlist / save-for-later - a visitor not ready to buy today has no way to signal intent and be brought back later.
- Basic `LIKE`-based search only, no ranking or typo tolerance.

## Purchase

**Today:** Guest-first checkout (ADR-002), single product per purchase session, Paystack-hosted payment page.

**Gaps:**
- No multi-item cart - a customer wanting two products makes two separate purchases, two separate checkouts, two separate emails. This is a real friction point for any future bundle/multi-product strategy.
- No coupon/discount application at checkout (ratified M4 candidate, `receipts.discount_pesewas` column exists and is unused).
- No installment/subscription option (explicitly out of scope per the Blueprint - "not modeled yet," would require a genuinely separate payments/charges table).

## Checkout

**Today:** Webhook-verified, idempotent, well-tested (this is the single most heavily-tested code path in the system). Automatic customer provisioning on first purchase (ADR-006).

**Gaps:** None found that this review considers a customer-facing problem - this stage is mature and has been independently verified at every milestone. The one open item (chargeback handling, ADR-010) is a business-continuity concern for the platform, not a customer-facing gap in the flow itself.

## Customer Account

**Today:** Real dashboard (M3): My Library, Purchase History, Receipts, Licenses, Account Security (password change, session management with the self-revocation protection independently verified in production).

**Gaps:**
- No profile beyond email - no name, no preferences, no communication settings beyond the site-wide newsletter subscription.
- No notification center - a customer has no way to see "your license was updated" or "a new resource related to your purchase is available" inside the dashboard itself; everything is email-only or nothing at all.
- Account creation is entirely purchase-triggered (deliberate, ADR-006) - a visitor who wants an account before buying (e.g., to build a wishlist) cannot get one. This is a direct structural tension with adding a wishlist feature: wishlist requires *some* form of pre-purchase identity, which the platform's own ratified architecture currently forbids.

## Library

**Today:** My Library correctly shows every owned purchase with download/receipt actions, correct empty/loading/error states, AR-009's ratified UX specification followed throughout.

**Gaps:**
- No progress tracking of any kind - for an ebook this is a minor gap (has the customer opened it?); for a hypothetical future course product it would be a hard blocker (a course with no progress tracking is not meaningfully a course).
- No re-engagement mechanic - a customer who bought once and never returned gets no nudge, no "here's what's new," nothing.

## Receipt

**Today:** Real generated PDFs, self-healing generation, both guest and authenticated download paths, independently production-verified at M2E and again at M3E.

**Gaps:** None found - this is one of the most mature, well-tested parts of the entire system.

## Downloads

**Today:** Mint-then-redeem single-use tokens, ownership-checked, revocation-synced (ADR-003), independently production-verified through M3E.

**Gaps:** None found for the current single-asset-per-purchase model. The one adjacent, already-known limitation (`deliveries` not seat-aware) only matters once a multi-seat product exists, which none does today.

## Long-term Engagement

**Today:** A one-time transactional email (receipt) and, separately, an opt-in newsletter a customer can subscribe to independently of any purchase. No connection exists between "I bought something" and "I now receive relevant follow-up content" - the purchase and the newsletter subscription are two entirely disconnected systems.

**Gaps (the largest cluster of gaps in the entire journey):**
- No purchase-triggered nurture sequence (a customer who buys the flagship ebook gets no automated "here's how to get the most out of it" or "here's your next recommended purchase" email).
- No re-engagement/win-back mechanic for lapsed customers.
- No loyalty, referral, or advocacy mechanic of any kind - a satisfied customer has no structured way to bring in another customer.
- No review/testimonial request after a purchase - the exact mechanic that would close the "no social proof" gap identified at the top of this journey (Visitor stage) is missing at the one point in the journey where it would be cheapest and most natural to collect (right after a customer has used and presumably valued their purchase).

---

## Summary: Where the Journey Breaks Down Most

The journey is **strong and mature from Purchase through Downloads** - this is the part of the platform that has received three consecutive milestones of dedicated engineering and independent review, and it shows. It is **weak at both ends**: nothing helps a cold Visitor trust the platform enough to buy (no reviews, no social proof), and nothing turns a satisfied Customer into a repeat buyer or advocate (no nurture, no referral, no re-engagement). This is the single clearest finding of this analysis and directly informs the Business Opportunity Report and M4 Scope Recommendation: **the highest-leverage next investment closes the two ends of the journey, not the already-solid middle.**
