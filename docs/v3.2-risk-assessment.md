# Version 3.2 - Sprint M4A - Risk Assessment

Every risk below is assessed against the real, current state of the platform and this planning sprint's own actual recommendation (M4 = Reviews + Coupons), not a hypothetical scope.

## Product/UX Risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reviews feature attracts low-quality or fake reviews | Low - purchase-gated submission (no review without a verified purchase) closes the most obvious abuse vector by construction | Medium - undermines the exact trust benefit the feature exists to create | Purchase-gating (built in from the start, not bolted on); manual admin moderation, matching existing precedent; no automated moderation needed at current volume |
| Coupon codes get shared/leaked beyond intended recipients | Medium (an accepted, already-reviewed risk per the ARB) | Low-Medium - discount revenue leakage, not a security or data risk | Explicitly accepted as non-blocking by the original ARB review; monitor redemption patterns post-launch, do not attempt to solve preemptively |
| The M5 re-sequencing (Retention/Engagement ahead of Support Ticketing) leaves real customer support needs unaddressed longer than the original plan intended | Low at current support-request volume (no evidence of unmet support demand was found in this review) | Low-Medium if wrong | Re-check support-request volume (consultation/contact form data, already tracked) at M4's close before committing to M5's scope - if volume has grown meaningfully, revisit the sequencing decision then, cheaply, before M5A begins |

## Technical Risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Admin-panel test coverage gap (a pre-existing condition, not new to M4) causes an undetected defect in the new review-moderation or coupon-management admin pages | Medium if not explicitly addressed (this exact pattern - an untested surface producing the acceptance review's own finding - has happened twice already, in M2 and M3) | Medium | Explicitly required in M4B's own Testing Strategy, named in this review's Implementation Strategy - do not let a third milestone repeat this pattern |
| M5's first-ever triggered/scheduled email behavior misfires (duplicate sends, missed sends) | Medium - genuinely new operational territory for this project | Medium-High for customer trust if it misfires visibly (e.g., duplicate nurture emails) | Build on the existing campaign system's already-proven duplicate-send-prevention pattern rather than inventing new trigger logic from scratch; update monitoring/alerting as an M5 deliverable, not an afterthought, per the Sprint Readiness Report's own explicit condition |
| Coupon-tax interaction is more complex than assumed once the VAT question resolves | Low for M4 itself (tax breakdown is empty regardless today) | Low now, Medium later if the interaction isn't revisited when VAT logic is eventually implemented | Explicitly flag the coupon-tax interaction as a required re-check item whenever the VAT question is finally resolved (cross-reference in whichever future milestone implements real tax computation) |

## Testing/Tooling Risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| No load/performance testing exists anywhere in this system (carried forward, unresolved, since the V3.0.2 Retrospective) | Low near-term (current traffic is well within headroom) | Medium-High if a growth-focused effort succeeds without a performance baseline first | Not M4's responsibility to resolve, but should not be silently carried into a fourth consecutive milestone without at least a scheduling decision - name a milestone (likely whichever one first drives a real traffic increase) that will establish the baseline |

## Business Risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| VAT-registration business question remains unresolved into a seventh consecutive planning pass | High (already true across six passes) | Low for M4 specifically (M4 doesn't need the answer), Medium-High for the business more broadly, and now also a governance-credibility risk (a question this project has explicitly recommended escalating five times without escalation actually happening) | This review recommends the Product Owner set an explicit decision deadline at M4 kickoff - not another documentation mention, an actual date |
| The M4/M5/M6 re-sequencing this roadmap recommends is implemented without the explicit governance amendment this review calls for | Low if this review's own recommendation is followed | Medium - would be a real, undocumented deviation from ratified architecture (ADR-005), the exact failure mode this project's governance process exists to prevent | Obtain explicit Product Owner ratification of the amendment (Reviews added to M4; M5/M6 re-sequenced) before M4A begins, per the Blueprint's own governance statement |
| Course-platform ambition is pursued prematurely due to pressure from this sprint's own "digital education platform" framing, overriding the evidence this review gathered | Medium - the framing itself creates a natural pull toward building the marquee feature immediately | High if it happens - XL effort, High risk, zero current demand evidence, and this project's own historical planning has already reached this conclusion twice independently | This review's Final Recommendation (see below) explicitly addresses this tension rather than deferring to the framing; M6's course-interest signal feature exists specifically to convert ambition into evidence before committing engineering effort |

## Deployment Risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| M4's two migrations (coupons, reviews) are deployed out of order relative to the Worker | Low (the exact same migration-first, Worker-second discipline has now been proven safe across three consecutive milestones) | Medium if it happened | Follow the identical, already-proven order-of-operations from M1/M2/M3's own deployment reports |
| Static-site deployment for any new Reviews/Coupons UI needs re-verification of the GitHub Pages mechanism | Low (already re-confirmed live as recently as M3D/M3E) | Low | Re-confirm, don't re-assume, at M4's own deployment time - matching this project's own established discipline |

---

## Overall Risk Posture

No risk identified above is severe enough to block approving this planning package or beginning M4A. The two risks worth actively managing *before* M4A begins, specifically because they are cheap to address now and more expensive to discover mid-build, are: obtaining the explicit governance amendment for the Reviews addition and M5/M6 re-sequencing, and setting a real deadline for the VAT decision rather than carrying it into a seventh pass. The one risk this review considers genuinely worth naming as a standing caution for the rest of Version 3.2, not just M4: the platform's own stated ambition ("digital education platform") creates real pressure to build the course-platform cluster before the evidence justifies it - this risk assessment, the Business Opportunity Report, and the Prioritization Matrix all converge on the same mitigation, which is to gather that evidence cheaply (M6) before spending XL effort on it (M7+).
