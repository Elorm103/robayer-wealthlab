# Version 3.3 Sprint M5B: Product Strategy Review

Evaluates whether M5A's proposed roadmap aligns with long term business vision, reviewing customer value, business value, conversion, retention, scalability, operational efficiency, and engineering effort.

---

## Customer Value

M5A's recommended scope delivers direct, measurable customer value to a specific, identifiable group: fifteen real people who already paid this business money and currently have no way to log in, see their purchase, or leave a review. This Board confirms this group is precisely fifteen distinct individuals, not an estimate, and confirms every one of them has a usable, provider-verified email address that a reconciliation flow could contact. This is not a hypothetical customer-value argument; it is a concrete, already-existing debt to real customers.

## Business Value

Correctly identified by M5A as an activation problem rather than a feature gap. This Board's own independent inspection agrees: two of the platform's four most recent feature investments (Milestone M4's coupon engine and review system) have zero real-world usage, and a newsletter system built even earlier (Version 2.1) has never been paired with a real campaign. Building a new revenue feature before activating these would compound, not resolve, the platform's actual business problem.

## Conversion

M5A correctly identifies that conversion work cannot be responsibly scoped without visitor analytics. This Board's own independent query refines the picture further: of ten non-verified purchase sessions, four are genuinely failed payments and six are abandoned or still pending, a materially more precise starting point than M5A's own "cannot be determined" framing suggested, though it does not change M5A's underlying recommendation that analytics should come before any deeper conversion investigation.

## Retention

M5A correctly scopes retention tooling out of M5, citing a two-account customer base too small to justify formal retention mechanics. This Board agrees, and adds one clarification not present in M5A's own documents: of the fifteen orphaned guest purchases, zero represent a repeat purchase (fifteen distinct people, fifteen distinct purchases, no duplicates), meaning this platform has never yet observed a single repeat purchase from any customer, guest or account-holding. This is a stronger, more precise version of a claim M5A made more loosely, and it further reinforces that retention tooling has no evidence base to be built against yet.

## Scalability

Not a near-term concern at current volume (17 lifetime purchases), consistent with M5A's own Architecture Assessment, independently confirmed by this Board's own review of the same codebase.

## Operational Efficiency

The recommended review-request email introduces the platform's first Cron Trigger. This Board confirms, via a search across this project's own pre-existing documentation (not merely M5A's own citation of the principle), that a "no new Cron Trigger without a specific, narrow, named reason" bar is a genuine, long-standing project convention, first documented well before this planning cycle. M5A's proposed use meets that bar: one query, one email template, tied to a specific, named customer-journey gap this project's own governance history predicted in advance.

## Engineering Effort

M5A estimates M5 as comparable in size to Milestone M3, smaller than Milestone M4. This Board finds this estimate reasonable given the account-reconciliation flow's dependency on an already-existing token mechanism (`customer_password_tokens`), independently confirmed present and reusable, requiring primarily a new matching step (email to historical purchase) rather than a new authentication primitive.

---

## Alignment With Long Term Business Vision

This Board finds M5A's recommended direction, activation and measurement before new feature construction, aligns with the long term vision better than any of the rejected alternatives would have. A marketplace vision (M7 in M5A's own roadmap) is better pursued from a larger, better-measured, better-retained single-seller base; a catalog-expansion vision (M6) is better pursued once real conversion data exists to plan bundles and upsells against. Sequencing M5 first is not merely defensible; it is the only sequencing this Board's own independent evidence review supports.

## Verdict

**No amendment required to the product strategy itself.** The Platform Fee Engine reasoning gap identified in `v3.3-m5b-architecture-approval-report.md` and detailed in `v3.3-m5b-platform-fee-strategy-review.md` is the only required correction, and it does not touch the product strategy's own core direction.
