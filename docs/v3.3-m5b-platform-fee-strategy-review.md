# Version 3.3 Sprint M5B: Platform Fee Strategy Review

Mandatory Phase 4 deliverable. Independently evaluates M5A's Platform Fee Engine Assessment, specifically checking whether it correctly distinguished two separate business capabilities: a Checkout Service Fee Engine and a Marketplace Commission Engine.

---

## Finding: M5A Did Not Cleanly Separate These Two Capabilities

M5A's own assessment (`v3.3-platform-fee-engine-assessment.md`) opens by describing the full feature request (percentage, fixed, or combined fee types, scope from global to campaign-level, financial-integrity treatment across checkout, webhook, receipts, and refunds), correctly identifies that this platform has no marketplace and no second-party seller, and concludes the whole request should be deferred. A later section ("If a Non-Marketplace Justification Emerges Later") briefly acknowledges that a smaller, single-seller checkout fee is a separate, lighter-weight possibility, but does not give it its own independent technical or business evaluation; it is dismissed in the same paragraph that defers the marketplace-scale version, using the marketplace's own absence as part of the reasoning even though a Checkout Service Fee Engine does not require a marketplace at all. This Board treats this as a real, if non-blocking, gap in reasoning quality, not a wrong conclusion.

---

## A. Checkout Service Fee Engine (Single Seller)

**Definition, for this review's own purposes:** an administrator-configurable fee (percentage, fixed, or combined) applied to any checkout on this platform, independent of any marketplace or seller-payout concept, most plausibly justified as a way to recover payment-processing cost or fund a stated operational cost, visible to the customer at checkout and on the receipt.

**Technical readiness, independently assessed:** this Board finds the underlying pattern already proven. Milestone M4's coupon engine already demonstrates the exact structural shape a Checkout Service Fee Engine would need: a server-computed adjustment to the charged amount, locked at checkout-session creation, verified against the payment provider's own confirmation at webhook time, and reflected as its own line in the receipt and PDF (`discount_pesewas`, confirmed present and populated correctly in production). A fee is the arithmetic inverse of a discount; the same locking, verification, and receipt-line pattern would apply, adding rather than subtracting from the base amount. **This Board's independent technical judgment: a Checkout Service Fee Engine is not technically difficult to build given the coupon engine's own precedent, and does not require the marketplace-scale scope (seller payout splitting, multi-party accounting) the full request describes.**

**Business justification, independently assessed:** no evidence in production supports this now. This Board found no record of a Paystack fee burden being flagged as a business concern, and the platform's own checkout completes only 63 percent of started sessions today (17 of 27), a conversion rate this Board would not want to risk further by adding a visible, unexplained extra charge to a GH₵39.00 purchase. **Verdict: technically ready, but not business-justified. Defer, but for a business reason, not a technical one.**

## B. Marketplace Commission Engine (Multi-Seller)

**Definition, for this review's own purposes:** a mechanism for the platform to take a percentage or fixed cut of a transaction on behalf of a third-party seller, requiring a revenue-split accounting model and a real seller-identity table.

**Technical readiness, independently assessed:** genuinely not ready. This Board confirmed, by direct schema inspection, that no `platform_users` table, no creator-payout mechanism, and no revenue-split logic exists anywhere in this codebase. This project's own architecture decision register (independently located and re-confirmed by this Board, not merely cited from M5A) gates the `platform_users` rename behind an explicit Milestone M7 entry condition requiring its own migration plan, with no timeline. **Verdict: not technically ready, and correctly so, since building it now would mean designing a revenue-split mechanism with no second party to split revenue with.**

## Why the Distinction Matters

Treating these as one capability, as M5A's document structurally does even while reaching the right practical conclusion, risks a future planning cycle re-deferring the Checkout Service Fee Engine on marketplace grounds that do not actually apply to it, or conversely, building the full marketplace-scale fee engine prematurely because "the fee engine" was approved as a single unit once a marketplace eventually exists. Keeping the two capabilities separately reasoned means a future business justification for the smaller Checkout Service Fee Engine (for example, a measured processing-cost burden) could be acted on independently of the marketplace milestone, without needing to wait for M7's own precondition.

---

## Determinations

- **Is the Checkout Service Fee Engine appropriate for a single seller platform?** Technically yes, in principle. Not currently justified by business evidence.
- **Should it remain deferred?** Yes, but on business grounds (no measured cost burden, real conversion risk at this platform's current checkout completion rate), not on technical-readiness grounds.
- **Does it belong in M5?** No. M5's own scope is already correctly focused on activation and measurement; introducing a new checkout-affecting change without the conversion data M5 itself is building would repeat the exact "measure before building" mistake M5 exists to correct.
- **Does it belong in M6?** Possibly, but only if a concrete business reason emerges (for example, if M5's own analytics data shows a Paystack fee burden worth passing through, or if a second product's pricing strategy specifically calls for it). Not recommended by default; recommended to be revisited with M6's own real data in hand, not scheduled speculatively now.
- **Does the Marketplace Commission Engine belong in a later milestone?** Yes, correctly scoped by M5A to the same milestone this project's own prior planning already designated (M7), for the same reason: no marketplace exists yet for it to apply against.

---

## Required Amendment to M5A's Documentation

`v3.3-platform-fee-engine-assessment.md` should be updated (or superseded by this document, at the release-authorizer's discretion) to reflect the separated treatment above, so a future reader is not left with the impression that a single-seller checkout fee and a marketplace commission engine share one readiness verdict. This is a documentation correction, not a scope change: this Board's own practical conclusion (neither belongs in M5) matches M5A's original conclusion exactly.

## Verdict

**Non-blocking for M5's overall approval.** M5's own scope does not include either fee capability, and this Board's independent review confirms that exclusion is correct regardless of how the reasoning is organized. The amendment is required before M5C begins, so the corrected reasoning is available to whichever future milestone eventually revisits the Checkout Service Fee Engine question.
