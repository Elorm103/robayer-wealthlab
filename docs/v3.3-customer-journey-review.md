# Version 3.3 Sprint M5A: Customer Journey Review

Reviews the complete customer lifecycle (Visitor, Landing Page, Product Discovery, Product Detail, Checkout, Purchase, Library, Reviews, Repeat Purchase, Retention), using the `anthropic-skills:ui-ux-pro-max` skill's priority-ordered UX framework (accessibility, touch and interaction, performance, layout, navigation) to structure the friction analysis, and real production data where it exists.

---

## Visitor

No visitor analytics exists (confirmed by direct code inspection), so this stage cannot be measured today: traffic volume, source, and device mix are all unknown. This is the journey's own first weak point: every downstream stage is analyzed relative to purchase-session counts, a lagging, imprecise proxy for actual visitor behavior.

## Landing Page

Server-rendered, no client-side hydration delay, honest featured-product and coming-soon sections. No friction found in this review specific to this stage.

## Product Discovery

Client-side keyword and topic-pill filtering across an already-loaded product list (`content-filters.js`). Entirely adequate for a two-product catalog; would need a real search index once the catalog grows past what a single loaded page can reasonably filter, but that is a future-catalog concern, not a current one.

## Product Detail

Server-rendered per product, includes structured data (JSON-LD, including the aggregate rating schema added in Milestone M4), an honest reviews section (currently showing the empty state on the one active product, confirmed live in production), and the coupon-entry field shipped in Milestone M4. No friction found specific to this stage beyond the review section's own current emptiness, which is a content and lifecycle problem, not a technical one (see Reviews stage below).

## Checkout

Sixty-three percent of created purchase sessions reach a verified purchase (17 of 27). Without visitor analytics, this review cannot determine whether the remaining 37 percent represent a real conversion problem (a friction point worth fixing) or an expected, benign rate of changed minds and declined cards. **This is the single clearest case in this entire review for why visitor analytics must come before further conversion-focused feature work**: building a checkout improvement without knowing where the drop-off actually happens would be guessing.

## Purchase

The webhook, receipt, and PDF chain is independently verified sound (Milestone M4's own closeout added a genuine end-to-end test for this exact path, including with a coupon discount applied). No friction found.

## Library

Only reachable by a customer with an account. **Fifteen of the seventeen people who have ever paid this business money cannot reach this stage at all**, since their purchase predates Milestone M1's customer-identity layer and was never reconciled to an account. This is the single most consequential friction point this review found in the entire journey: it is not a rough edge, it is a closed door, for the large majority of the platform's real customers.

## Reviews

Purchase-gated correctly (a real, account-linked, verified purchase is required, confirmed by code inspection and by Milestone M4's own independent review). Zero reviews exist in production. Two compounding causes: first, the account gap above means most historical customers cannot reach the review form at all; second, no automated prompt of any kind (email, dashboard banner, post-download nudge) ever invites an eligible customer to actually leave one. Milestone M4B's own Customer Journey Validation named this exact gap in advance ("Reviews ships before the review-request trigger that would actually drive people to use it"); this review confirms, with real production data, that the predicted gap is now a real, measured zero.

## Repeat Purchase

Not observed in production (no evidence either of the two account-holding customers has purchased twice), and not structurally possible for most customers regardless, since there is only one active product to repurchase. This stage cannot be meaningfully improved until a second product exists.

## Retention

No retention mechanic exists beyond the passive dashboard itself (no email sequence, no win-back trigger, no loyalty accrual). At 2 account-holding customers, this is appropriately unbuilt for now; retention tooling built ahead of a customer base large enough to retain would be premature investment.

---

## Weak Points, Ranked by Severity

1. **The account gap (Library stage).** Closes access to Reviews, Purchase History, and every future account-based feature for 88 percent of real customers.
2. **No visitor analytics (Visitor and Checkout stages).** Makes every conversion decision downstream a guess rather than a measurement.
3. **No review-collection trigger (Reviews stage).** Already predicted by this project's own prior governance history; now confirmed in production as a real zero.
4. **No lifecycle email (Retention stage).** Lower severity than the above three given the current account base size, but compounds the account gap: even customers who do have an account receive no further contact after their welcome email.

## Recommended Improvements

- Build a "claim your purchase" reconciliation flow: a guest purchaser who never had an account (matched by the email their purchase was made under) can create a password and be linked, after the fact, to their own historical purchase or purchases.
- Ship lightweight visitor analytics (Cloudflare Web Analytics is the natural fit given the platform's existing Cloudflare-native architecture, requires no backend change, no new data-processing obligation beyond what the platform already handles).
- Add one triggered email, sent a short, fixed number of days after a verified purchase, inviting the customer to leave a review, linking directly to the product page's own review form. This closes the exact gap Milestone M4B's Customer Journey Validation predicted in advance.

See `v3.3-feature-inventory.md` for how these are scoped as candidate features, and `v3.3-m5-recommended-scope.md` for which of them this review recommends including in M5 itself.
