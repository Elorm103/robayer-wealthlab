## Per-milestone detail

### M1 — Customer Identity Foundation

- **Dependencies:** None (foundational).
- **Risks:** Low. Entirely proven-pattern reuse (`admin_sessions` → `customer_sessions`). The one design risk the ARB identified — an ambiguous scope around self-registration — is now closed by ADR-006.
- **Outstanding decisions:** None.
- **Prerequisites:** None beyond the ratified architecture itself.
- **Recommended action:** Begin immediately.

### M2 — Ownership Layer: Orders, Licenses, Receipts

- **Dependencies:** M1 (`customer_id` needed on new rows).
- **Risks:** Low-Medium. Receipt/license-key generation-under-concurrency risk is mitigated by reusing the already-proven `purchase_reference` collision-retry pattern — not a new risk. The revocation-synchronization requirement (ADR-003) is small and well-specified.
- **Outstanding decisions:** **VAT-registration status** — a factual question about this business's actual tax registration, not an architectural one. This document cannot answer it. Until answered, `tax_breakdown`'s schema can and should still be built (per ADR-008, the structure costs nothing to leave unused), but the computation logic that populates it should not be finalized against a guess.
- **Prerequisites:** Obtain the VAT-registration answer from the business before finalizing (not before *starting*) the tax-computation logic specifically.
- **Recommended action:** Begin schema and non-tax-computation work immediately; flag the VAT question to the business owner in parallel so it's answered before that specific piece of logic needs to be finalized.

### M3 — Checkout Auto-Provisioning + Dashboard MVP

- **Dependencies:** M1, M2.
- **Risks:** Medium-High — this is the first genuinely customer-visible surface in the roadmap, and the ARB specifically flagged the account-creation prompt's framing as the single biggest UX risk in the entire architecture (get it wrong and the conversion benefit the whole guest-first design is built around evaporates). This risk is unchanged by ratification — it is a risk to be managed during implementation and validated with real usability testing, not one that documentation alone can retire.
- **Outstanding decisions:** None at the architecture level — empty states, destructive-action confirmations, and prompt frequency are now all specified (Deliverable 5 of the ratified Blueprint).
- **Prerequisites:** M1, M2 complete.
- **Recommended action:** Ready to begin once M1/M2 ship; budget explicit usability validation time, not just engineering/design review, given the ARB's specific concern here.

### M4 — Coupons/Promotions Activation

- **Dependencies:** M1.
- **Risks:** Low. Design already fully specified in `docs/v3-database-design.md`, adopted unchanged. The one known weakness (multi-account bypass of `first_purchase_only`) is an accepted, documented risk, not a blocker.
- **Outstanding decisions:** None.
- **Prerequisites:** M1 complete.
- **Recommended action:** Ready to begin once M1 ships; may proceed in parallel with M2/M3 if resourcing allows, since it has no dependency on either.

### M5 — Support Ticketing

- **Dependencies:** M1 (customer-linked tickets), M3 (dashboard surface to host the Support page).
- **Risks:** Low — almost entirely a direct copy of the already-proven `consultation_requests` pattern.
- **Outstanding decisions:** None.
- **Prerequisites:** M1, M3 complete.
- **Recommended action:** Ready to begin once M3 ships.

### M6 — Notifications + Email Reliability

- **Dependencies:** M1 (notifications need a customer to belong to).
- **Risks:** Medium — the narrow-scope Cron Trigger (ADR-007) is this project's first-ever scheduled background job; nothing in this codebase has run on a schedule before. This is new operational territory regardless of how well-scoped the decision is, and deserves proportionate testing care in a staging environment before production use.
- **Outstanding decisions:** None at the architecture level — the scope question the ARB flagged (ADR-007) is now resolved. The remaining work is procedural: the two operational documents must be updated as part of this milestone's own deliverables, not treated as optional follow-up.
- **Prerequisites:** M1 complete.
- **Recommended action:** Ready to begin once M1 ships, with the explicit condition that `docs/monitoring-and-alerting.md` and `docs/backup-and-recovery.md` updates are included in this milestone's own definition of done.

### M7 — Marketplace Schema Activation (Creators/Affiliates)

- **Dependencies:** M1–M4 (a creator's product needs orders/coupons/licenses to already work correctly for a single-owner catalog before adding a second seller).
- **Risks:** High for the rename step specifically (unusually wide FK blast radius across the schema, per ADR-011) — not high for the rest of the milestone's scope, which is already thoroughly designed in `docs/v3-database-design.md`.
- **Outstanding decisions:** **The `platform_users` rename migration/rollback plan does not yet exist.** This is genuine, substantive planning work — deciding on a maintenance-window approach vs. a staged/dual-read approach, and validating it — that this documentation-only governance sprint is not scoped to produce. It requires its own focused planning effort, likely involving DevOps/database engineering directly, before M7 can begin.
- **Prerequisites:** M1–M4 complete, plus the migration plan above, plus the cross-role dashboard UX question (a customer who is also a creator) resolved as part of M7's own design scope (per ADR-004, explicitly deferred to this milestone, not resolved earlier).
- **Recommended action:** **Requires Replanning** — schedule a dedicated planning pass for the `platform_users` rename specifically before treating M7 as ready to begin, even though M1–M4 will likely be complete well before M7's turn in the sequence.

### M8 — Go-Live Readiness Review

- **Dependencies:** M1–M6 (M7 is explicitly *not* a Live-Mode blocker, per the ratified Blueprint's own Executive Recommendations — the marketplace is a Version 4.0 capability, not a launch requirement for the existing single-catalog business).
- **Risks:** Low engineering complexity, high stakes — this is a review gate, not a build milestone. The real risk is organizational: the temptation to skip or rush it under launch pressure.
- **Outstanding decisions:** None at the architecture level — chargeback handling (ADR-010) is now fully specified. It is not yet implemented, which is expected (M8 is sequenced after M1–M6, and chargeback handling ships as part of M8's own scope).
- **Prerequisites:** M1–M6 complete.
- **Recommended action:** Ready to begin, in the sense that nothing further needs deciding — but it cannot meaningfully *start* until its dependencies are actually done. Treat this as "Ready with Conditions" where the condition is simply "M1–M6 ship first," not any open design question.

## Recommended implementation order

Unchanged from the ratified Blueprint's own dependency graph, with one addition: **the M7 migration-plan work should be started in parallel, early**, precisely because it's the one item in this entire roadmap flagged as "Requires Replanning" rather than merely "Ready with Conditions" — starting that planning work now, alongside M1–M4, means M7 doesn't become a late-discovered bottleneck when its turn arrives.

```
M1 ─┬─→ M2 ─→ M3 ─┬─→ M5
    ├─→ M4 ────────┤
    └─→ M6         └─→ M8 (after M1–M6 all complete)

M7's migration-plan work: start now, in parallel — M7 implementation itself
still waits on M1–M4, but the planning gap should not.
```

## Outstanding risks carried forward (unresolved by this ratification, by design)

These are not failures of this ratification sprint — they are risks the ratified architecture explicitly identifies as *not* resolvable by documentation alone, consistent with this sprint's own scope (documentation and governance, not implementation or business-fact-finding):

1. **VAT-registration status (M2 prerequisite)** — a factual question for the business, not an engineering or architecture question.
2. **`platform_users` rename migration plan (M7 blocker)** — genuine technical planning work, not yet done.
3. **The account-creation prompt's real-world conversion impact (M3 risk)** — can only be validated with real usability testing during implementation, not settled in advance by design review alone.
4. **Coupon multi-account fraud (accepted risk, M4)** — deliberately not mitigated now; monitor for real abuse post-launch, per the ratified architecture's own risk-acceptance stance.

## Final Approval Statement

**Architecture Ratified — Approved for Implementation, with M7 excluded pending its own replanning.**

Seven of eight milestones (M1–M6, M8) are Ready or Ready with Conditions, where every condition is either already satisfied by this ratification (M1, M3, M4, M5) or is a small, well-understood procedural step to complete alongside the milestone's own work (M2's VAT question, M6's documentation updates, M8's dependency wait). None of these seven milestones has an open architectural question left to resolve — the Architecture Review Board's findings have been fully integrated, traced, and closed via the Amendment Register and Decision Register.

**M7 is the one genuine exception**, and it is excluded from this "approved for implementation" statement specifically and narrowly: its underlying design (`docs/v3-database-design.md`, plus this ratification's ADR-004/ADR-011 additions) is sound and approved, but the operational migration-safety planning it requires has not yet been done, and this documentation-and-governance sprint is correctly scoped not to produce it. This is exactly the outcome the ratified Blueprint's own Executive Recommendations already anticipated: M7 was never intended to gate Live Mode, so this exclusion has no bearing on the platform's readiness to launch.

**Executive summary of remaining work before Sprint 2 begins:** none, for M1–M6 and M8 — Sprint 2 may begin immediately against the ratified architecture as written. The one piece of genuinely outstanding work — a reviewed migration/rollback plan for the `platform_users` rename — should be scheduled as its own short, focused planning effort, run in parallel with early Sprint 2 work rather than sequenced after it, so it is resolved well before M7's turn in the roadmap arrives.
