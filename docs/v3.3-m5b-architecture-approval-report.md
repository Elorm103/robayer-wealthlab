## Executive Summary

**M5A's recommended scope is directionally sound and its production evidence is accurate.** Every figure this Board independently re-queried (product count, customer count, purchase and revenue totals, the account-linkage gap, review and coupon usage, newsletter subscriber count) matched M5A's own reporting exactly, with zero discrepancy found.

**This Board found one genuine gap in M5A's own reasoning, not in its data:** the mandatory Platform Fee Engine assessment did not cleanly separate a Checkout Service Fee Engine (a single-seller capability) from a Marketplace Commission Engine (which genuinely requires a marketplace). M5A's own document discusses both under one heading and reaches the same practical conclusion (do not build either in M5) for what reads as one blended reason, when in fact the two capabilities have different technical readiness and different business justification thresholds. See `v3.3-m5b-platform-fee-strategy-review.md` for the full, separated treatment this Board performed.

**This Board also independently strengthened, rather than merely confirmed, two of M5A's central claims**, using data M5A itself did not query: every one of the fifteen orphaned guest purchases has a real, non-empty, provider-verified `customer_email`, and every one belongs to a distinct person (fifteen distinct email addresses, zero overlap with the two existing customer accounts). This closes a possible objection M5A left implicitly open (whether reconciliation is technically viable for all fifteen cases, not just most).

**Final decision: Option B, M5 Scope Approved with Required Amendments.** See Section "Summary of Required Amendments" below.

## Summary of Required Amendments (Blocking for M5C Start, Not for This Approval)

1. **Produce a separated Checkout Service Fee Engine versus Marketplace Commission Engine evaluation** before M5C implementation begins, using the distinction and conclusions in `v3.3-m5b-platform-fee-strategy-review.md`, so a future milestone revisiting either capability has the correct, separately-reasoned evidence to work from rather than one blended dismissal.

## Non-Blocking Observations

- M5A's checkout-conversion framing ("cannot be determined without analytics") should be updated to reflect the more precise breakdown this Board found (4 failed, 6 pending, not one undifferentiated 37 percent gap) - a documentation refinement, not a scope change.
- M5A's own document filename (`v3.3-m5-recommended-scope.md`) does not match this review's own brief, which expected `v3.3-recommended-m5-scope.md`. The content is complete and accurate; only the filename differs. Noted for documentation consistency, not a functional concern.

## Final Decision

## Option B: M5 Scope Approved with Required Amendments

**No Blocking finding prevents approving M5's overall scope** (account reconciliation, visitor analytics, triggered review-request email). The one Required Amendment is a correction to the Platform Fee Engine assessment's own reasoning structure, not a change to what M5 should build. Every non-blocking observation should be tracked and addressed during M5C, not before it begins.

**Is the project ready to proceed to Sprint M5C: Activation, Analytics and Customer Reconciliation Implementation?**

**Not directly to full implementation. A short, focused correction should come first**, consistent with how this project has handled every prior milestone's own required amendments (Milestone M4's own schema-correction amendment being the closest precedent): M5C should begin with, or be immediately preceded by, the separated Platform Fee Engine reasoning named above. This is small enough to fold into M5C's own opening work, not a reason for a separate lettered sprint. Once that correction exists, implementation may proceed.
