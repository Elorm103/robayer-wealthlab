# Version 3.3 Milestone M5C: Platform Fee Architecture Clarification

**Resolves the one Required Amendment from Sprint M5B's Architecture Approval Report:** the mandatory Platform Fee Engine assessment did not cleanly separate two distinct business capabilities. This document is that separation, produced before any M5C feature work began, per the sprint's own Phase 1 instruction. It supersedes the blended treatment in `docs/v3.3-platform-fee-engine-assessment.md` for the specific question of whether these are one capability or two; that document's other content (the original mandatory assessment) remains accurate and is not otherwise revised.

**No code was written for this document. No fee engine of either kind is implemented in Milestone M5.**

---

## A. Checkout Service Fee Engine

**Purpose:** an administrator-configurable fee (percentage, fixed, or combined) applied to a checkout on this single-seller platform, independent of any marketplace or seller-payout concept. The most plausible real-world justification is recovering a portion of payment-processing cost, or funding a stated operational cost, as a transparent, separate line at checkout.

**Architectural boundaries:** this capability, if ever built, belongs entirely within the existing checkout and receipt pipeline, not a new subsystem. It would follow the exact structural pattern the coupon engine (Milestone M4) already established: a server-computed adjustment to the charged amount, locked at checkout-session creation, verified against the payment provider's own confirmation at webhook time, and reflected as its own line in the receipt and PDF receipt (the same role `discount_pesewas` already plays, with the arithmetic sign reversed). It would require no `platform_users` table, no seller-identity concept, and no revenue-split accounting, since there is only one seller on this platform.

**Business justification:** not currently supported by evidence. Sprint M5B's own independent review found no record of a Paystack processing-fee burden being flagged as a business concern, and this platform's checkout currently completes only 63 percent of started sessions; adding a visible, unexplained extra charge risks that conversion rate for no measured benefit. A future business reason (for example, a Paystack fee burden made visible by Milestone M5's own new analytics work) would need to exist before this capability is worth building.

**Future roadmap placement:** not scheduled for Milestone M5 or Milestone M6 by default. Tracked as a candidate for Milestone M6's own scope, contingent on a concrete business reason emerging from Milestone M5's analytics data (see `v3.3-m5b-roadmap-validation.md`'s own recommended addition to M6's candidate list). Not scheduled speculatively.

---

## B. Marketplace Commission Engine

**Purpose:** a mechanism for this platform to take a percentage or fixed cut of a transaction on behalf of a third-party seller, requiring a genuine revenue-split accounting model.

**Architectural boundaries:** this capability cannot exist without a real seller-identity model first. It depends entirely on the `platform_users` table this project's own architecture decision register (ADR-011) has already gated behind a Milestone M7 entry condition, requiring its own migration plan, with no timeline set. Building any part of a commission engine before that precondition exists would mean designing a revenue-split mechanism with no second party to split revenue with, the exact premature-design mistake this project's own governance history (Milestone M4B's independent review of the original coupon schema) has already caught and corrected once.

**Business justification:** none exists today. This platform has exactly one seller (itself), confirmed by direct schema inspection during both Sprint M5A and Sprint M5B. There is no partner, creator, or third-party seller relationship this capability would apply to.

**Future roadmap placement:** Milestone M7, and not earlier, matching this project's own pre-existing designation for the marketplace cluster of features (course platform, affiliate program, multi-seller commerce). This capability should be designed as part of, not before, whichever milestone actually introduces a real marketplace, since its own scope (fee type, minimum and maximum bounds, product and category scoping) only has real meaning once multiple sellers exist to apply it against.

---

## Why the Distinction Is Documented Permanently, Not Just Resolved for M5

A future planning cycle revisiting "the platform fee engine" as a single named feature would be at risk of re-deferring the Checkout Service Fee Engine on marketplace grounds that do not actually apply to it, or conversely, treating the Marketplace Commission Engine as ready simply because a lighter-weight sibling capability was once approved. Keeping these separately reasoned, in this dedicated document, means either capability can be picked up independently, on its own merits, whenever a real business reason for it exists.
