# Version 3.3 Sprint M5A: Platform Fee Engine Assessment

Mandatory Phase 3 deliverable. Independently evaluates a configurable Platform Fee Engine against the platform's actual current architecture and business model, not against a generic marketplace template.

---

## What Is Being Asked For

An administrator-configurable service or processing fee, layered on top of (or blended into) a product's price, with support for percentage, fixed-amount, and combined fee types; enable and disable toggles with effective dates; minimum and maximum fee bounds; scope at the global, product, category, and future-campaign level; visibility choices (baked into the displayed price versus shown as a separate line at checkout, on the receipt, and on the PDF receipt); and full financial-integrity treatment across checkout, the payment session, the webhook, receipts, refunds, reporting, and accounting.

## Why This Feature Exists as a Concept

A platform fee is the mechanism a marketplace uses to take its own cut when it processes a payment on behalf of a third-party seller, distinct from the payment processor's own transaction fee (which Paystack already charges automatically and which this platform has no visibility into or control over at the application layer). It is a real, standard, necessary primitive once a platform has more than one seller receiving payouts.

## Independent Verification Performed

- **Confirmed via direct schema inspection: this platform has exactly one seller.** There is no `platform_users` table, no creator-payout mechanism, no revenue-split logic anywhere in `backend/database/schema.sql`. Every one of the 17 verified purchases in production paid the same entity: Robayer WealthLab itself.
- **Confirmed via this project's own documented history: a multi-seller marketplace has been considered and deliberately deferred at least twice already.** ADR-011 (independently re-confirmed present in this project's architecture decision register during Milestone M4B's own review) gates the `platform_users` rename behind a Milestone M7 entry condition, explicitly "requires an explicit, reviewed migration plan," with no timeline. `docs/v3-marketplace-architecture.md` and related planning documents describe a marketplace vision that has never been built.
- **Confirmed via the receipts schema: `discount_pesewas` already exists and is now used (Milestone M4) for coupons, but there is no equivalent `fee_pesewas` or `platform_fee_pesewas` column anywhere.** Adding one would require a new migration, exactly the kind of schema-correctness question this project's own M4B review caught once already for a different feature (the coupon schema's `platform_users` reference).

## What a Platform Fee Would Actually Do at This Business's Current Scale

The only entity a platform fee could be charged to today is the platform's own single product line, meaning it would function as a disguised price increase or a visible checkout surcharge on the one GH₵39.00 book this business sells. There is no third party to charge a platform fee to. Adding a visible "service fee" line to a GH₵39.00 checkout, at a business currently converting 63 percent of started checkouts, introduces real conversion risk (a customer seeing an unexplained extra charge at the final step is a well-documented cause of cart abandonment) for a revenue mechanism that has no actual second party to apply it against.

## Does This Belong in M5

**No.** There is no marketplace, no third-party seller, and no revenue-splitting need today. Building the full fee-type, scope, visibility, and financial-integrity surface this assessment was asked to evaluate would be substantial engineering effort (a new migration, checkout changes, webhook changes, receipt and PDF changes, refund-calculation changes, reporting changes) in service of a business model this platform does not have.

## Recommended Milestone

**Defer to whichever future milestone actually introduces a multi-seller marketplace or a genuine second-party payout relationship** (consistent with this project's own existing ADR-011 and Milestone M7 designation for exactly this kind of marketplace-dependent work; see `v3.3-roadmap.md`). This assessment recommends the Platform Fee Engine be designed as part of, not before, that same marketplace milestone, since the fee engine's own scope (percentage or fixed, minimum and maximum bounds, product and category scope) only has real meaning once there are multiple sellers whose payouts the fee is computed against. Designing it in isolation now would risk exactly the kind of premature, unvalidated schema this project's own M4B review already caught once (a design built ahead of the business model that would give it meaning).

## If a Non-Marketplace Justification Emerges Later

If the business ever wants a simple, single-seller "checkout service fee" (for example, to recover payment-processor costs directly, unrelated to any marketplace), that is a much smaller, separately-justifiable feature: a single configurable percentage-or-fixed value, no seller-scoping, no revenue-split accounting. This assessment does not recommend building even that smaller version speculatively; it should be scoped only if and when a concrete business reason for it exists (for example, a measured Paystack fee burden the business wants to pass through), which no current evidence in this review supports.

---

## Verdict

**Platform Fee Engine: not recommended for M5. Not recommended for M6. Recommended to be scoped alongside the marketplace milestone this project has already designated (M7 in its own prior planning), if and when that milestone is actually approved.**
