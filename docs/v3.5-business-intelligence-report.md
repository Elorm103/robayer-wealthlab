# Version 3.5: Business Intelligence Report

## Purpose

Documents every analytical computation added this milestone (Phases 3, 4, 7, 8, 10), what real table each draws from, and — per the brief's explicit "do not fabricate forecasts, base calculations on actual data" instruction — exactly what the one forecasting feature does and does not claim.

## Sales analytics (Phase 3)

All range-scoped (`from`/`to`, defaulting to the last 30 days), all computed live from `purchase_sessions`/`payment_transactions`/`coupon_redemptions`:

- **Daily revenue** — `SUM(amount_pesewas)` grouped by `date(verified_at)`, status `verified` only.
- **Top selling products** — grouped by `product_slug`, joined to the live `products.title` (falls back to the order's own snapshotted title if the product was since deleted), ranked by revenue, limit 10.
- **Coupon usage** — `coupon_redemptions` joined to `coupons`, grouped by code: redemption count and total discount given.
- **Sales by payment channel** — the one genuinely new data source this milestone taps: `payment_transactions.gateway_response` is a raw, verbatim JSON blob of what Paystack reported at webhook time (see `commerceService.ts`'s `handlePaymentWebhook()`); `json_extract(gateway_response, '$.data.channel')` pulls the real channel (`mobile_money`, `card`, etc.) without needing a new column. Verified live: the real production data currently shows 18 mobile-money orders against 1 card order, consistent with Version 3.4.3's own finding that mobile money is this market's dominant channel.

## Customer analytics & experience metrics (Phases 4, 8)

Also range-scoped:

- **New customers / account creations** — `COUNT(*)` on `customers.created_at` in range (the same field for both metrics; a "new customer" and an "account creation" are the same event in this schema).
- **Returning customers (in range)** — a verified purchase in range by a customer who already had an earlier verified purchase, using the same tie-broken `EXISTS` pattern `analyticsService.ts`'s `repeatPurchasesInRange()` already established (verified_at has one-second resolution; `id` breaks ties).
- **Password recoveries** — `email_log` rows with `template = 'customer-password-reset'` and `status = 'sent'` in range. Deliberately excludes `customer-welcome` (a new account's first password setup is not a "recovery") and `customer-purchase-reconciliation` (a distinct "recover an old purchase" event, already visible as the Recover Purchase flow) — see Version 3.4.3's Email Delivery Root Cause Report for why these three templates exist and mean different things.
- **Most downloaded products** — `deliveries` grouped by `product_slug`, filtered to `last_download_at` in range.
- **Review submission rate** — `product_reviews` created in range, divided by verified purchases in the same range. A ratio above 100% is possible and meaningful (a customer can review a product purchased in a prior period).
- **Customer lifetime value** — lifetime `SUM(amount_pesewas)` across all verified purchases with a customer_id, divided by the count of distinct customers with at least one such purchase. Not range-scoped (a lifetime measure scoped to 30 days would misrepresent what "lifetime" means).
- **Average time to first review** — `AVG(julianday(review.created_at) - julianday(purchase.verified_at))` joined on customer and product, across every review that has ever been submitted. A purchase with no review yet contributes no data point (never treated as "infinite" or zero).
- **Average time to purchase** — `AVG(julianday(verified_at) - julianday(created_at))` across all verified purchase sessions — i.e. how long a checkout typically takes from session creation to payment confirmation, not a marketing "time to decide to buy."
- **Repeat purchase rate** — customers with ≥2 verified purchases, divided by all customers with ≥1 verified purchase, lifetime.
- **Top referrers** — not implemented. No referrer field exists anywhere in this schema; see the Architecture Report.

## Revenue intelligence & financial dashboard (Phases 7, 10)

Lifetime unless stated otherwise:

- **Best selling product / highest revenue day** — straightforward `MAX`/`ORDER BY` aggregates, both `null` (rendered as "no verified orders yet") until at least one verified purchase exists.
- **Fastest growing product** — the last 30 days vs. the 30 days before that, per product, ranked by percent growth. Deliberately excludes any product with zero revenue in either window — a product with no prior-period orders has an undefined growth rate, not an infinite one, and showing it as "fastest growing" would be a fabricated signal, not a real one. Correctly returns "not enough data yet" today, since this catalog's real order history doesn't yet span two full comparison windows for more than one product.
- **Average discount / revenue lost to coupons** — `AVG`/`SUM` of `discount_pesewas` across verified purchases that used a coupon.
- **Gross vs. net revenue** — gross = net + total discounts (the pre-discount subtotal); net = `SUM(amount_pesewas)`, the actual amount charged. Paystack's own per-transaction processing fees are not included in either figure — they exist only inside each transaction's raw `gateway_response` blob, not as a structured, reliably-present field across every historical row, so including them would mean silently mixing real and estimated figures. This is stated explicitly rather than left ambiguous.
- **Most discounted product** — the product with the highest total discount given, among coupon-linked verified purchases.
- **Revenue by month** — last 12 months, grouped by `strftime('%Y-%m', verified_at)`. Rendered as a chart using the existing line-chart component (a genuine time series, unlike payment channel).

### Sales forecast — what it is and is not

The brief: *"Sales Forecast (simple trendline). Do not fabricate forecasts. Base calculations on actual data."*

Implementation: the average month-over-month absolute change in real revenue across the trailing months (currently up to 12), projected one month forward from the most recent real month. Deliberately **not shown at all** when fewer than 3 real months of revenue exist — extrapolating a trend from 1–2 data points is a guess, not a trendline, and the brief's own instruction rules that out. Verified live: with this catalog's current single month of real revenue history, the dashboard correctly displays "Not enough monthly history yet for an honest trendline" rather than inventing a number. This will begin showing a real forecast once a second and third month of verified revenue exist.
