Section 1

Welcome to Robayer WealthLab

Robayer WealthLab publishes practical, Ghana-focused financial education: digital guides that help ordinary Ghanaians understand saving, investing, and building wealth in terms that actually apply to their lives here, not generic advice written for another country's financial system.

The Affiliate Programme exists because the best way most people discover this kind of content is a recommendation from someone they already trust, and for many of your followers, that person is you. This Academy exists so that before you start sharing your link, you actually understand what you're recommending, how the mechanics behind it work, and what's expected of you as someone representing Robayer WealthLab to your own audience.

Section 2

What Robayer WealthLab Offers

Robayer WealthLab is a publisher of short, practical digital guides on personal finance and investing, written specifically for a Ghanaian audience. The current catalogue includes guides on Ghana's Treasury Bills, the Ghana Stock Exchange, building savings from small, irregular amounts, turning mobile money into a real savings habit, and legitimate remote-work income, alongside a bundle that combines the core investing titles at a discount.

Every guide is built around the same idea: plain-language explanations, worked examples using real Ghanaian institutions and amounts, and step-by-step actions a reader can actually take, not just theory.

Section 3

Understanding Our Digital Guides

Each guide is a digital product: delivered instantly after purchase, readable on a phone or computer, with no physical shipping involved. That matters for how you talk about them: a reader can start reading within minutes of buying, from anywhere in Ghana or abroad, which is worth mentioning when you recommend one.

Before recommending any specific title, read its product page in full, and where possible skim the guide itself. A recommendation built on real familiarity with the content is more convincing, more honest, and far less likely to mislead a buyer about what they're actually getting. The marketing resources page keeps current product descriptions and approved assets in one place.

Section 4

Who Our Products Are Designed For

Robayer WealthLab's readers are mostly Ghanaians taking their first real steps with money: someone who's never bought a Treasury Bill and doesn't know where to start; someone who wants to understand the stock exchange before risking real cedis; someone whose income is irregular and wants a savings system built for that reality, not for a fixed monthly salary.

Matching the right guide to the right person is part of promoting honestly. Someone who's never saved consistently doesn't need the stock exchange guide as their first purchase; someone already investing might skip the beginner savings guide entirely. Understanding this audience is what separates a genuinely useful recommendation from a generic "check this out" post.

Section 5

Your Role as an Affiliate

An affiliate is not an employee, a reseller, or an official spokesperson for Robayer WealthLab. You don't set prices, you can't offer discounts that haven't been published, and you don't speak for the company in any official capacity. What you are is an independent recommender: someone who shares a link, and earns a commission when that link leads to a genuine purchase.

That distinction matters for how you present yourself. Say "I recommend this guide" or "I've found this useful," not "we," when talking about Robayer WealthLab, and don't imply an employment or partnership relationship beyond being an approved affiliate. Clarity here protects both your audience's trust and your own credibility.

Section 6

How Affiliate Links Work

Every approved affiliate gets a unique referral code, built into a trackable link from your Referral Links page for any product in the catalogue. That link is what you actually share (in a post, a message, a video description), and it's how a purchase gets connected back to you.

The tracking is entirely server-side, so it can't be faked or spoofed from your end, and there's nothing extra you need to configure. Get the link, share it honestly, and the system does the rest.

Section 7

How Referrals Are Attributed

When someone clicks your link, a first-party cookie records that visit against your code for 30 days. If they complete a purchase within that window, using the same browser they clicked in, the sale is attributed to you automatically. If someone clicks more than one affiliate's link before buying, the most recent click gets credit; timing matters more than being first.

This has a real limit worth understanding honestly: if a visitor clears cookies, switches devices, or waits past 30 days between clicking and buying, the referral can't be recovered. There's no way around that technically, so the honest strategy is simply making your link easy to find and click right when someone's genuinely ready to act, not chasing workarounds.

Section 8

How Commissions Are Generated

Your commission rate is shown on your dashboard once approved, and applies to the sale price of any eligible purchase made through your tracked link within the 30-day attribution window described above. A commission is generated automatically the moment a qualifying purchase completes; you never need to submit or claim anything.

A commission can be reversed if the underlying purchase is refunded, found to be fraudulent, or later identified as a self-referral. Commissions move through pending, payable, and paid stages as they clear; your Earnings page always shows exactly where each one stands.

Section 9

What Happens After Someone Clicks Your Link

The moment someone clicks your link, the click is recorded against your affiliate code and the 30-day attribution cookie is set in their browser. From there, they browse the site like any other visitor: reading the product page, maybe looking at other guides, maybe leaving without buying at all. That's normal; not every click converts, and it never will.

If they do buy within the 30-day window, the checkout system resolves your referral fresh at that exact moment (checking it's still a real, currently-approved code, and not a purchase on your own account) before locking the sale to you. If they never come back, nothing happens: no commission, no penalty, just a click that didn't convert this time.

Section 10

How to Promote Our Products Effectively

The content that converts best is useful on its own, with or without a purchase at the end of it. A short post on "three things I learned about Treasury Bills" that happens to link to the guide for more detail works far better than a bare "buy this" message.

Formats worth trying: a personal story about a financial decision you made and what you wish you'd known sooner; a summary of one useful idea from a guide, credited honestly, with a link to read the rest; a comparison of a few approaches to a common problem (e.g. "three ways Ghanaians save for irregular income") where a Robayer WealthLab guide is one relevant option among your points, not the entire post. Quality and honesty consistently outperform volume.

Section 11

Social Media Promotion: Facebook, TikTok, YouTube, WhatsApp & More

Each platform has its own norms worth respecting. On Facebook and TikTok, disclose that a link is an affiliate link where the platform expects it (a simple "affiliate link" or "#ad" is usually enough). This protects your credibility and is often a platform requirement, not just good practice. On YouTube, put the link and a spoken disclosure in videos that mention a product, not just buried in a description no one reads.

WhatsApp is powerful for reaching people who already know and trust you: status updates and genuine one-to-one conversations work; mass-forwarding your link to every contact or group you're in does not, and reads as spam. Across every platform: avoid link-only posts with no context, avoid repeating the same link in a short window, and never buy followers, use bot engagement, or run fake-engagement schemes to inflate reach.

Section 12

Writing Trustworthy Promotional Content

Trustworthy content is specific, not vague. "This guide changed my finances" says nothing; "this guide walked me through opening my first CSD account step by step" tells your audience exactly what to expect and shows you actually engaged with the material. Specificity is also what separates an honest recommendation from generic hype.

Always be clear about what a guide covers and who it's for, mention that your link is an affiliate link, and never present your personal opinion as an official Robayer WealthLab statement. If you haven't read a guide, don't write as if you have. A vague recommendation is easy for your audience to spot, and it costs you more credibility than it earns you in commission.

Section 13

What Affiliates Should NOT Claim

Never claim guaranteed returns, guaranteed income, guaranteed commissions, or guaranteed approval of any kind. Robayer WealthLab's guides teach principles and strategies for building wealth responsibly; they don't guarantee any specific financial outcome, and neither should you. Completing this Academy does not guarantee your application will be approved, and you should never tell a prospective affiliate otherwise either.

Also prohibited: misrepresenting what a guide contains (claiming a beginner guide covers advanced trading strategies it doesn't), impersonating Robayer WealthLab or its founder, creating fake urgency ("only 2 spots left," "price doubles tomorrow") that isn't true, and any self-referral: buying through your own link, or having someone close to you do it on your behalf. Self-referrals are checked for automatically and voided.

Section 14

Responsible Promotion of Financial & Investment Education

This section is the one most worth taking seriously. Financial content carries real consequences for the people who act on it, so the bar for honesty here is higher than for most affiliate categories. A reader who buys a savings guide because of your post is trusting your judgement with their actual money.

Be honest about risk where a guide discusses it (the stock exchange guide, for instance, is explicit that share prices can fall as well as rise), never suggest a guide is financial advice tailored to one person's situation, and never pressure someone into a purchase they seem uncertain about. Responsible promotion protects your audience and, in the long run, protects your own reputation as someone worth listening to.

Section 15

Understanding Clicks, Conversions & Commissions

Three numbers matter on your dashboard, and they mean specific things. A click is one visit that came through your link, whether or not it leads anywhere. A conversion is a click that led to a completed, paid purchase within the 30-day window. Your conversion rate is simply conversions divided by clicks, a rough measure of how well your specific audience and content match what you're promoting.

A commission is the cedi amount you earn from one conversion, calculated from your commission rate and that purchase's sale price. Most affiliates convert a small fraction of their clicks; that's normal across this kind of programme, not a sign something's wrong.

Section 16

Monitoring Your Affiliate Performance

Once approved, your Overview page shows clicks, conversions, conversion rate, and revenue generated at a glance. Referral Links is where you get your trackable link for any product. Earnings breaks down every commission by status and lets you request a payout once your payable balance clears the minimum threshold shown there.

Check in periodically rather than constantly: clicks and conversions update as real visits and purchases happen, so there's nothing to refresh in real time. Tracking which content or channel is actually converting for you is the fastest way to improve your results over time.

Section 17

Building Trust Instead of Spamming

The affiliates who do best over time are the ones their audience already trusts, not the ones who post the most links. Spam (unsolicited bulk messages, comment-flooding, mass DMs, repeatedly posting the same link) is never allowed, damages your own credibility, and typically converts poorly anyway, since people can tell the difference between a genuine recommendation and a copy-pasted pitch.

Building trust is slower but compounds: consistently useful content, honest disclosure, and recommendations that actually fit the person you're talking to. That's what turns an occasional click into a following that keeps coming back to what you share.
